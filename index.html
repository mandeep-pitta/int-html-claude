A. Quota performance and track record

Top priorityR1, R2The job description's first requirement, and the resume's biggest omission.
A1

"Walk me through your quota and attainment for each year since 2020: number, percentage, and where you ranked on the team."

Strong answer

Exact numbers given without hesitation, e.g. "FY24: $1.5M quota, closed $1.8M, 120%, #2 of 9." Knows how they compare to peers. Matches what references say.

Red flags

"Usually around quota." Switches to pipeline or activity numbers. Blames the territory every year.

Follow-ups

  • What made the difference in your best year?
  • What happened in your weakest year, and what did you change?
  • Split of new logo vs. expansion vs. renewal?
  • Any year below 80%? Why?
A2

"Your resume mentions multi-million-dollar opportunities but lists ACVs of $200K–$600K. Help me reconcile that. What's the largest deal you personally closed, by ACV and by TCV?"

Testing

Accuracy, deal scale, honesty.

Strong answer

A clear explanation, e.g. "a 3-year, $1.8M TCV with EOG," and a precise account of their own role vs. the team's.

Red flags

Counts pipeline or team deals as their own. Gets defensive.

Follow-ups

  • How was it structured: multi-year, prepaid, ramped?
  • Who did you negotiate with in procurement?
  • What did you give up to get it signed?
A3

"Have you been to President's Club or earned other recognition? If not, what's kept you from it?"

Strong answer

Specific awards, or an honest, reflective explanation.
A4

"How accurate is your forecast in the quarter you call it? Tell me about a deal that slipped."

Strong answer

Knows their accuracy rate. Clear about commit vs. best case. Owns the slip and describes what they changed afterward.

Red flags

"I never miss my forecast." Blames only the customer.

B. Enterprise deal execution

R2The job description asks for someone who can lead large-scale transactions and build consensus across a buying committee.
B1

"Pick your most complex win, maybe Baker Hughes, EOG or the State of Louisiana. Walk me through it from first touch to signature."

Probe with MEDDPICC

  • MWhat quantified outcome did they sign up for?
  • EWho signed? When did you first meet them?
  • DHow did you learn, and shape, the evaluation criteria?
  • DWhat was the decision process and who was in it?
  • PWalk through procurement, legal and security review.
  • IWhat was the compelling event? The cost of doing nothing?
  • CWho was your champion, and how did you test them?
  • CWho were you up against, and how did you win?

Strong answer

Names, titles and dates. Clearly ran the deal and shaped the buying process.

Red flags

Generic process talk. All "we", no "I". Can't name the economic buyer. The SE or partner did the real work.
B2

"Tell me about a large deal you lost. What would you do differently?"

Strong answer

Honest self-critique (single-threaded, missed the economic buyer, poor qualification) with concrete changes since.

Red flags

Blames only price, product or the customer.
B3

"Describe a deal where the buying committee disagreed, say Security wanted one thing and Infrastructure another. How did you build consensus?"

Follow-ups

  • How did you map the org?
  • How many contacts were engaged at close?
B4

"How do you run a proof-of-concept so it ends in a purchase instead of a science project?"

Strong answer

Success criteria agreed up front, a mutual action plan, exec sign-off before starting, time-boxed and tied to a decision date.

Red flags

Free, open-ended POCs driven by technical evaluators with no business sponsor.
B5

"Tell me about a time you sold a multi-year commitment or an enterprise license agreement."

Testing

Readiness for Cloudera's larger, multi-year subscription structures.

D. Data, analytics and AI domain

Key gapR3Don't expect SE-level depth. Test baseline literacy, curiosity, preparation and learning speed.
D1

"What do you know about Cloudera, what we sell and who we compete with? What made you interested?"

Strong answer

Clear homework: a hybrid data platform across on-prem and cloud, an open lakehouse (Apache Iceberg), data engineering, warehousing, streaming, AI, shared security and governance. Names Databricks, Snowflake and the hyperscalers' native services.

Red flags

"A big data company," or "the Hadoop company" with no update. No preparation.
D2

"In plain business terms, explain the difference between a data warehouse, a data lake and a lakehouse, and why a customer would care."

Strong answer

Warehouse: curated, structured data for BI. Lake: cheap storage for raw data of any type, flexible but harder to govern. Lakehouse: lake scale with warehouse performance and governance on open formats. Value: one copy of data, lower cost, analytics and AI on everything.

Red flags

Bluffing with buzzwords. A partial answer plus an honest learning plan is acceptable.
D3

"How does your identity security background translate into selling a data platform?"

Strong answer

Draws real parallels. Identity governance ↔ data governance. Access control ↔ fine-grained data policies. Audit and compliance (SOX, HIPAA, state privacy) ↔ lineage and auditing. A CISO relationship helps clear security review in data deals.
This is the candidate's best bridge. A thoughtful answer here is a strong positive.
D4

"Pick one of your energy customers. What data and analytics use cases do you think they have, and who in the organization owns them?"

Strong answer

Seismic and subsurface data, rig and pipeline sensor data, predictive maintenance, production optimization, emissions reporting, supply chain, AI for drilling. Owners: CDO, VP Digital, Head of Data Engineering, OT/IT leaders.

Follow-up

"Do you know anyone in those organizations outside Security?" This tests whether the network transfers.
D5

"What's the hardest technical concept you've had to learn fast for a sale? How did you learn it?"

Testing

Learning agility, the main thing that offsets the domain gap.
D6

"How are enterprises thinking about AI and their data right now? What's driving the urgency?"

Strong answer

Data readiness for AI, private AI on your own data, governance concerns, cost control, hybrid deployment for sensitive data.

E. Cloud, containers and Kubernetes

GapR3Cloudera's data services run on Kubernetes across on-prem and cloud. The AE needs a working conversational grasp.
E1

"At a high level, what are containers and Kubernetes, and why would an enterprise data team care?"

Strong answer

Containers package an app with its dependencies so it runs the same anywhere. Kubernetes runs and scales them. For data: elastic, isolated workloads that move between on-prem and cloud and use hardware efficiently.

Red flags

No idea and no curiosity.
E2

"A CIO says, 'We're cloud-first, so why would we need a hybrid platform?' How do you respond?"

Strong answer

Asks first: what's driving it, cost, regulation, latency, data gravity, existing investments. Then positions hybrid: sovereignty, cloud cost control and repatriation, no lock-in, one governance layer, portable workloads.

Red flags

Argues with the customer. Leads with features.
E3

"How have you worked with AWS, Azure or GCP in a deal: marketplace, committed spend, co-selling?"

Testing

Awareness of cloud marketplace motions. Likely a gap, so look for awareness rather than experience.
E4

"How do you partner with a Solutions Engineer? Where's the line between your job and theirs?"

Strong answer

The AE owns the business case, economic buyer, process and close. The SE owns technical validation. They plan and review calls together, and the AE can hold a first-level technical conversation.

I. Logistics

R1Ask every candidate these the same way.
  • "This role requires up to 50% travel, about 2 weeks a month. Can you meet that requirement?"
  • "Are you legally authorized to work in the U.S.? Will you now or in the future require sponsorship for employment visa status?"
  • "What are your compensation expectations for base and OTE?" Follow local pay-transparency and salary-history laws.
  • "What territory or accounts would excite you most?"
  • "What's your notice period and earliest start date?"
  • "Do you have any non-compete or non-solicit obligations?"

Don't ask about age (or graduation year as a stand-in for it), family, marital status, health, religion, national origin or any other protected characteristic.

Mock exercise (Round 4)

Given the domain gap, a practical exercise is the best predictor. Send the scenario 48–72 hours ahead.

Scenario · fictional

Gulf Coast Energy Co.

A Houston-based Fortune 500 upstream and midstream energy company, and a long-time Cloudera customer running an older on-prem deployment for seismic, sensor and production analytics. The renewal is in 7 months. A new Chief Data Officer, hired from a cloud-native company, is pushing cloud-first, and one business unit is piloting a cloud-native competitor. The CISO is worried about moving sensitive operational and regulatory data to public cloud. Cloudera wants to renew, expand into hybrid and public cloud data services and AI use cases, and attach professional services.

Part 1 · Account plan · 20 min + 10 Q&A
  1. Situation assessment and key risks
  2. Stakeholder map: CDO, CIO, CISO, BU leaders, procurement, possible champions
  3. Value hypothesis and use cases
  4. Competitive strategy
  5. 90-day plan, including use of SE, CS, partners and executives
  6. Deal structure: renewal, expansion and PS
Part 2 · Discovery role-play · 20 min

An SE or the hiring manager plays the new CDO: skeptical and leaning cloud-first.

Mid-call challenge: "Honestly, the competitor is 30% cheaper. Why shouldn't I just move?" Watch for composure and a value-based reframe rather than discounting.
